About Me

a little about myself

Anamika Yadav

My path into design didn't start with a Figma file — it started with noticing bad experiences before I had a name for them: a form nobody filled out, a clinic queue that never moved, an app that looked fine but frustrated everyone using it.

Studying UX gave that instinct a method: talk to people first, then design. I'm early in this career — a Master's in Human-Centered AI & Experience Design in progress, one internship at AkkoRent, and a small handful of independent projects like QCare and ClarityLens. What I'm building is the habit of a rigorous process: research before assumptions, and design decisions I can actually explain, not just defend.

Design Process

a repeatable process, not a straight line

In practice this loops — testing sends me back to define, iteration sends me back to research. This is the order it settles into by the time something ships.

01 Research

Understand first

Interviews and contextual inquiry to find what's actually happening.

02 Define

Sharpen the problem

Synthesis to separate symptoms from root causes.

03 Ideate

Generate widely

Divergent sketching, kept rough so weak ideas die early.

04 Wireframe

Structure first

Low-fidelity flows to pressure-test logic before style.

05 Prototype

Make it real

Interactive builds made specifically to be tested.

06 Test

Watch, don't defend

Usability sessions with real or representative users.

07 Iterate

Fix what's found

Revisions driven by evidence, not opinion.

08 Launch

Ship & listen

Handoff with documentation and a measurement plan.

Published Paper

A Review Study of Cognitive Load in Website and Mobile Application User Interface Design

Published in the Journal Publication of International Research for Engineering and Management (JOIREM), Vol. 04, Issue 4, April 2026 — ISSN (O) 3107-6696. Co-authored with Vasudha, Assistant Professor, World University of Design. The paper reviews how intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load shape usability, and the design strategies — visual hierarchy, content chunking, progressive disclosure — that help reduce unnecessary mental effort in UI design.
